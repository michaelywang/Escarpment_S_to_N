§ 01 The Knee Question

Is S→N actually easier on knees?

Conventional wisdom in the Catskill backpacking community is that the Escarpment Trail "should" be hiked south-to-north. The reasoning given is usually vague — "save the views for last," "the climbs are gentler that way." For a group with knee concerns, vague isn't good enough. The honest answer requires looking at which direction climbs vs. descends each of the three or four sections that actually matter for joint stress.

The headline finding: yes, S→N is meaningfully easier on knees — but not by as much as the trail community implies, and it comes with real tradeoffs. The single biggest factor is the Blackhead east face. It is the most eroded stretch of the entire trail, with rock scrambles, loose footing, and at least one ledge where packs come off. You want to climb that face, not descend it. S→N has you climbing it. N→S has you descending it.

Original Plan
North → South
NY-23 to Schutt Rd. Front-loads Windham climb, descends Blackhead east.
vs.
This Plan
South → North
Schutt Rd to NY-23. Climbs Blackhead east, descends Windham gradually.

How much better, really?

The knee-stress delta is dominated by Blackhead's east face. That single 1.1-mile section is so much rougher than anywhere else on the trail that flipping its direction does most of the work. SectionHiker's account specifically calls out the east descent as "where the storm took its toll on the trail with many areas heavily eroded" — in a guide written by an experienced AMC trip leader. AllTrails reviews echo this: "the trail starts to descend rapidly" out of Blackhead, with rock scrambling required.

Trade against this: N→S has the modest Stoppel/Arizona descent and the west-face Blackhead descent on its side. These are real, but neither is as eroded or as long as the east face.

Bottom line: S→N reduces total knee-stress by roughly 30–40% in the worst stretches, with most of that gain concentrated in the Blackhead east section and the Windham final descent. For a hiker with a known knee issue, this is a meaningful improvement. For a hiker without one, the difference is felt mostly as "less afraid going down" rather than "less tired."

§ 03 What S→N Costs You

The tradeoffs.

It is not a free lunch. Three real costs come with reversing the direction.

Cost #1

Friday-night logistics get harder

In the N→S plan, you start at NY-23 and lodging in Windham (5–10 min from the trailhead) sits next to your start. Friday night is simple: drop cars, eat dinner, sleep, walk to the trail.

In the S→N plan, you start at Schutt Road, which is the southern end. The Friday-night options near here are: (a) car-camp at North-South Lake — but that's the campground with the strict 9 PM check-in cutoff that's basically impossible to make from Chatham after work, or (b) lodge in Tannersville/Hunter/Haines Falls. Those towns have lodging, but it is more limited and pricier than Windham. Or: still lodge in Windham (where the variety is) and eat the 35-minute drive to the southern trailhead Saturday morning.

Cost #2

Blackhead climb on Day 2 with tired legs

In S→N, the Blackhead east face climb (~1,300 ft in <1 mi, the trail's hardest single climb) lands on Sunday morning with a pack that's been carried for ~14 miles already. Even though we'll have lightened by some food and water weight, this is a significant Day-2 effort.

In N→S, the same section is a Saturday-afternoon descent with relatively fresh legs (and full of consequence — but not as cardiovascularly demanding). S→N front-loads Day 2 with the trail's hardest climb. If anyone has tired legs Sunday morning, it shows up here.

Cost #3

The "best views last" rule cuts both ways

The conventional wisdom says S→N saves the best views for the end. It's true that North Point, Newman's Ledge, Inspiration Point, and Artist Rock are the most photogenic stretch — and in S→N you hit them on Saturday afternoon when light is best for photography (the cliffs face east). But you pay for it: by the time you reach Windham High Peak's summit Sunday afternoon, you've been on the trail for 22 miles and may not have the energy to enjoy it. S→N gives you better Saturday photos and a more anticlimactic Sunday finish. N→S gives you a strong Sunday morning of cliff-walking and a quiet finish at Schutt Road.

Cost #4 (smaller)

Saturday water plan changes

In N→S, Batavia Kill Lean-To (mile 8.6) is a critical mid-day water stop right before Blackhead. In S→N, Batavia Kill is at mile ~15.3 — well past the camping target — and Dutcher Notch spring (mile ~12.4 from south) becomes the key Saturday water source instead. Both flow but slowly in late August. Plan stays workable; just different stops.

§ 05 Friday — Pre-Staging

Friday in the S→N world.

The Friday-night problem from the original plan now affects the start instead of the end. The same 9 PM gate at North-South Lake still applies. The sensible move is similar but mirrored: lodge in Windham (best lodging pool), then drive south to start. Or lodge in Tannersville/Hunter to be near the start. Both options work; Windham is more comfortable and more available.

Recommended Friday plan (Windham lodging, drive south Sat AM)

  • 4:00–5:00 PM — Depart Chatham, NJ.
  • 7:00–8:00 PM — Arrive Windham area. Drop 2+ cars at NY-23 / Cross Road parking (the finish in this plan).
  • 8:00–8:45 PM — Convoy 35 min south to Schutt Rd parking. Drop 2 cars at the start. Note: do not attempt to enter North-South Lake Campground for parking after 9 PM; the Schutt Rd day-use lot just outside the campground is fine for overnight parking.
  • 9:30 PM — Drive back to Windham lodging (35 min).
  • 10:30 PM — Late dinner / sleep.
  • Sat 6:00 AM — Caravan from Windham south to Schutt Rd in 1 car. The hike begins from the start, ~7:30 AM.

Alternative: lodge near the start

  • Tannersville / Hunter — has motels (Hunter Mountain Lodging, Black Bear Lodge) and Airbnbs. Closer to Schutt Rd start (~15 min drive). More limited and seasonal pricing on a Labor Day-adjacent weekend.
  • Haines Falls — small town, very limited lodging, no notable restaurants. Useful only for cabin rentals.
  • Saugerties / Catskill — full towns, plenty of options, but adds 30–40 min to Saturday morning drive.
  • Verdict: still recommend Windham lodging unless someone in the group strongly prefers being near the start. The 35-minute Saturday morning drive is acceptable; the lodging quality is better.
Same

Park 2+ cars at finish (NY-23 in this plan) and 2 cars at start (Schutt Rd). Sunday: 2 drivers from finish drive ~35 min south to retrieve their cars. Same 1.5-hour shuttle gap.

Dutcher Notch area · alternative camp option

If the group is moving fast and a closer-to-water site is preferred, push 1.5 mi past Arizona to Dutcher Notch. Spring 0.4 mi north (down the Dutcher Notch trail) for water. The notch itself is below 3,500 ft, so legal dispersed camping is possible. This is the recommended Saturday-night camp for S→N: it puts the brutal Blackhead climb first thing Sunday morning when legs are freshest.

Why camp at Dutcher Notch?

It positions Sunday morning's hardest climb at peak energy

Camping at Dutcher Notch means waking up at the foot of Blackhead's east face — the trail's hardest climb. Doing it first thing Sunday on fresh legs is ideal. Water is available 0.4 mi off-trail at the Dutcher Notch spring. Sunday total then becomes ~11.4 mi with the Blackhead climb up front and a long, gentle Windham descent at the end.

§ 06 Contingencies for S→N

If things go sideways in this direction.

Sat · slow pace

If we're at Newman's Ledge (mile 5.3) and it's already 1:30 PM

The day is rolling and slow. Camp at Arizona col (mile 10.4) instead of Dutcher Notch — saves 2 mi but makes Sunday morning's Blackhead climb start with a 2-mile descent first. Workable.

Sun · stuck on Blackhead

If the Blackhead east climb takes longer than 90 min

Don't worry. The rest of the day is gentle. Even a 2.5-hour Blackhead climb still puts the group at Batavia Kill by 11:30 and NY-23 by 6:00 PM. The bigger risk is bonking on the climb — eat a real breakfast, eat snacks at every break, and don't let anyone push through silent dehydration.

Sun · injury on Blackhead

If someone gets hurt on the east face

The bailout is back down to Dutcher Notch then 1.9 mi east on the Dutcher Notch Trail to Storks Nest Rd. This is the easiest off-ridge bailout on the whole trail. After Blackhead's summit, the next bailout is at Big Hollow Rd via the Batavia Kill Trail (mile 15.3, ~1.5 mi descent).

All scenarios

The Windham descent is the safety net

The final 3.4 mi from Windham summit to NY-23 is the friendliest 3.4 miles on the entire trail. Even an exhausted, dusk-running group can make this stretch in headlamps without much risk. This is the structural advantage of S→N for tired hikers: the easiest section of the trail is the section you finish on.

§ 08 Verdict

Should you flip the direction?

Recommendation
If anyone in the group has known knee issues, descending stairs at home is uncomfortable, or has had a meniscus / patellar / IT-band history, flip to S→N. The reduction in worst-case knee stress is real and meaningful, and the costs (slightly trickier Friday logistics, harder Sunday morning, later finish) are manageable.
Recommendation
If knees are not a concern in this group, stick with N→S. The original plan finishes earlier on Sunday, gets the harder day done first, and saves a quiet finish at Schutt Rd as the natural end of the trip. The "saving the views for last" argument doesn't outweigh the logistical and pacing advantages of N→S for a knee-healthy group.
Hybrid option

Distribute knee-vulnerable hikers strategically

If only 1–2 people in the group have knee concerns and the rest are fine, you can stay with N→S and have those individuals take Blackhead's east descent very slowly with the strongest pole-supported pace. Alternatively, those individuals can use the Black Dome Range Trail bailout at mile 6.1 to skip the Blackhead summit entirely (descending to Big Hollow Rd, getting picked up by a shuttle, and meeting the group at North-South Lake the next day). This is a bigger intervention than flipping the whole group's direction, but for one person with severe knee issues, it might be the right call regardless of the group's chosen direction.

§ 09 Decision Points

What we need to pin down.

To make the direction call

  • Survey the group on knee history. Specifically: anyone with surgeries, recurring knee pain on long descents, or chronic IT-band/patellar issues? One affirmative answer probably tips this to S→N.
  • Are anyone's poles questionable? Going N→S with a knee-questionable hiker requires confident pole technique on Blackhead east. If poles are missing or technique is poor, S→N has a wider safety margin.
  • Group's tolerance for hard-climbing-on-day-2. S→N puts the trail's hardest climb on Sunday morning. If anyone in the group is the type to be physically destroyed by Saturday's effort and unable to climb fresh on Sunday, that's an argument against S→N.

If we choose S→N, do this

  • Book Windham lodging — same recommendation as N→S.
  • Plan the Saturday-morning 35-min drive south to Schutt Rd. Coffee + breakfast for the road. Aim to be on trail by 7:30 AM.
  • Saturday-night camp target: Dutcher Notch area if possible (positions Sunday's Blackhead climb at peak energy); fallback Arizona col.
  • Pre-stage finish-line bags in the cars at NY-23 (not Schutt Rd) — clean clothes, sandals, drinks for the post-hike shuttle wait.
  • Eat a real Sunday breakfast at camp — oats, bars, calories. Don't start the Blackhead climb on coffee alone.
This plan vs. the N→S plan
Same trail, same length (23.9 mi), same trip dates, same group, same shuttle vehicles. Differs only in direction and the cascading effects on Friday lodging logistics, Saturday/Sunday mileage split, and Sunday timing.
